I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the University of Pisa (39th cycle), carrying out my research at the High Performance Computing Laboratory (HPC-Lab) of ISTI–CNR. I am supervised by Massimo Coppola (ISTI–CNR) and co-supervised by Vincenzo Lomonaco (LUISS University), and I also work with the ATLAS Lab (University of Pisa / LUISS University).

My research asks how AI-based controllers can manage structured applications across heterogeneous, dynamic cloud–edge–IoT–space infrastructures. I design adaptive orchestration that combines reinforcement learning, distributed systems and serverless / HPC platforms, and I build benchmarks that make such controllers comparable. A complementary line of work on continual learning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) lets foundation-model controllers keep adapting as requirements change.

Before my PhD I spent several years in industry as an embedded Android engineer at Lenovo and MobileXP, working on camera HAL and device drivers.

Research

My doctoral work, AI-driven resource management for structured parallel applications across the extended cloud continuum, has converged on three connected topics.

01

Structured applications & learning-based autoscaling

Structured applications are modelled as compositions of parallel skeletons with explicit bottleneck laws, which gives controllers low-variance runtime features to learn from. I implemented a farm skeleton on OpenFaaS over Kubernetes with a Gymnasium-based monitoring and control layer exposing queue, timing and QoS metrics. On this testbed, an RL-based autoscaler of the worker pool compensates for serverless orchestration delays and achieves near-perfect deadline satisfaction. It builds on an earlier hybrid model in which local agents manage partitions of the continuum under partial observability, coordinated by a global orchestrator.

Papers: PDP 2025 PDP 2026

02

ContinuumBench: an extended continuum platform & benchmark

Grounded in an Earth observation workflow that fuses satellite imagery with IoT soil telemetry, I extended the ECLYPSE simulator to a four-layer cloud–edge–IoT–space continuum with contact-scheduled satellite visibility and store-and-forward buffering. ContinuumBench closes the control loop with dynamic workload arrivals, worker elasticity and node failures, and introduces completion-aware accounting that scores late and unfinished tasks as deadline misses. Across nine controllers, rankings depend on the evaluation regime: completion-blind metrics mask deadline misses, while completion-aware accounting separates autoscalers with matched throughput by up to 50× in deadline misses. A learning-ready control layer on top standardises observations, action schemas, feasibility masks and traces, turning benchmark runs into reproducible datasets.

Papers: FRAME 2026 PAA 2026

03

Continual adaptation & foundation-model control

With the ATLAS Lab I work on continual learning and efficient adaptation of foundation models: calibration of continual learners, optimisers with adaptive drift control, and continual parameter-efficient fine-tuning. Bringing these methods back to the continuum, I study foundation-model controllers adapted across sequential management regimes, with candidate actions constrained by a simulator-validated safety boundary, and measure when continual adaptation forgets the rare but decisive scaling decisions of earlier regimes. Earlier, with the BioRobotics Institute of Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna, I developed continual policy distillation for RL controllers in soft robotic in-hand manipulation.

Papers: FRAME 2026 AIxIA 2026 HHAI 2025 I-RIM 2025 CVPRW 2024 RoboSoft 2024

Next: learning objectives from operator intent

Today's controllers optimise objectives whose weights are fixed in advance, while operators state prioritised and sometimes conflicting requirements as service-level agreements. My next steps move from selecting actions under a fixed objective to learning the objective from operator intent, and keeping that translation current as requirements evolve.

Continual intent translation

A strategic language model maps sequences of intents and telemetry to control parameters, while a deterministic tactical controller enforces feasibility within sub-second epochs. Continual instruction tuning with parameter-efficient adapters preserves earlier translations as new intents arrive.

Execution-grounded benchmark

Sequential SLA-to-control tasks scored by their closed-loop optimality gap in simulation rather than by parameter equality, reporting forgetting, forward transfer and behaviour on infeasible requests. Intent streams and reference traces will be released with ContinuumBench.

Feedback-driven multi-agent orchestration

Testing when feedback-triggered orchestration beats open-loop control across continuum tiers, under varying workload, connectivity and cross-tier objective conflicts, with ablations of the cross-tier mediator and trust gate.
